# xMustard v0.1.1 (unreleased)

Not tagged yet. A fix release for one release-affecting bug in v0.1.0. The fix is
built on `parity/ws-fix-03`
([build plan, WS-FIX-03](../plans/2026-09-25-parity-build-plan.md#ws-fix-03--production-streaming-secret-redactor-for-evidence-capture)).

## Fixed: evidence capture is on and redacted

In v0.1.0 no production build wired a streaming secret redactor into capture. So
`POST /api/workspaces/{id}/evidence/capture` answered `503 redaction_unavailable` to
every request. As a result, Pi's built-in tool reduction, masking and compaction fell
back to Pi's own behavior, and no hook body (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Pi, OpenCode)
could be captured. Only the e2e build (`-tags xmustard_e2e`) captured, and its
redactor replaced one test marker.

In v0.1.1 capture is on in every build, and every capture is redacted:

- The tool output streams through the shared WS-05 secret rules (`redact.Writer`)
  before any of it reaches the spool. The retained original, its pages, search inside
  it and the projection the client receives therefore all hold the redacted text,
  with `[REDACTED:<rule>]` in place of each secret. The rules are the ones `why_failed`
  applies to command output: the token and key patterns, key-aware secret fields, and
  the literal values of the daemon's secret-named environment variables.
- The redactor streams. It buffers 128 KiB and decides it 8 KiB at a time, reading
  each window with the 33 KiB lookahead after it, which is the longest span any rule
  reads. A secret split across writes is seen whole, and the stream is never
  buffered. A window's scratch and output are sized by the 8 KiB it decides, not by
  how many secrets they hold: on 16 MiB of input the redactor's live heap peaks at
  0.4 MiB at most, on input that is nothing but secrets, under a quarter of the
  2 MiB capture window.
- The output of a secret path (`.ssh/`, key files, `.netrc`, `.npmrc`, `.pypirc`,
  `.dockercfg`, `.env` and `.env.*` other than templates) is refused with
  `422 secret_path` and not retained. Every path the capture names is checked: the
  caller's `path`, and every path field of the hook body's tool input and response.
  The client keeps its own result.
- Capture still fails closed. A server without a redactor answers
  `503 redaction_unavailable`, and a redactor that fails answers
  `503 redaction_failed`. In both cases nothing is retained.

See [docs/SECURITY.md](../SECURITY.md#evidence-capture-redaction-ws-fix-03) for the
rules and their limits.

## Measured (Linux x86_64 build box, AMD Ryzen 5 3500X)

- `redact.Writer` alone (`BenchmarkWriter16MiB`, 32 KiB writes): 142 MB/s on a stream
  with a secret pair every 4 KiB, and 33 MB/s on one that is nothing but secrets
  (`secret=x` lines). It allocates 0.27 MB and 0.47 MB per 16 MiB. The first build of
  this fix, which decided a whole 128 KiB buffer per window, ran at the same speed
  but allocated 0.95 MB and 5.6 MB; its CPU time was 1 to 4% higher.
- Peak live heap of the redactor, 16 MiB of one repeated unit
  (`redact.TestWriterMemoryIsBounded`): 0.17 to 0.37 MiB, the most for an 8-byte
  secret environment value repeated. The first build measured 3.3 MiB on that input,
  and 2.0 to 2.1 MiB on `secret=x` and `"secret":"x"` repeated.
- The capture route, end to end against a real `xmustard-api`. Each figure is the
  range of three interleaved runs of three builds: this release, the first build of
  the fix, and this release with a pass-through redactor (a temporary build tag, not
  committed). The host's load average was about 6 to 10 on 6 cores.

  | Workload | This release | First build | Pass-through |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 6 sequential raw 16 MiB captures, a secret pair every 4 KiB | 31.4 to 33.0 MB/s | 32.3 to 33.5 MB/s | 33.3 to 39.3 MB/s |
  | 4 sequential raw 4 MiB captures of nothing but secrets | 6.5 MB/s | 6.1 to 6.5 MB/s | 6.5 to 6.7 MB/s |
  | 200 Claude hook bodies of 256 KiB, 4 at a time | 40.1 to 44.1 MB/s | 39.8 to 40.1 MB/s | 46.8 to 48.9 MB/s |
  | API peak RSS (`VmHWM`) over the three workloads | 36.4 to 37.0 MiB | 37.7 to 39.4 MiB | 44.2 to 44.8 MiB |

  Idle RSS was 18.7 to 19.0 MiB for all three. The dense workload is bound by the
  shell reducer, not the redactor: the pass-through build is no faster. At a load
  average of 3 to 4, the first build measured 43.2 to 45.9 MB/s on the raw workload
  and 79.5 to 81.5 MB/s on the Claude workload, with peak RSS 37.4 to 40.0 MiB.

## Tests

- The Go suites pass on the build box, including the streaming redactor's tests
  (every golden secret split at every write, window and buffer boundary; the densest
  secret units; a large-input allocation bound) and the capture route in a default
  build.
- The Pi adapter e2e (`scripts/e2e/pi-adapter.sh`) passes in part: 9 of its 18 e2e
  tests. Its 45 unit tests and the WS-24 capture suite pass (built-in tools through
  capture with the markers redacted, masking and compaction). Nine tests of its first
  suite fail, and fail the same way without this fix: they need a multi-page result from `impact` with no
  arguments, and since v0.1.0's automatic registration baseline the fixture repository
  has no changes against its baseline, so that call returns no handle. The fix is a
  large-result fixture for `impact`, owned by the Pi adapter workstream (WS-24; see
  the build plan). Until it lands, the e2e is recorded as partially passing.

## Compatibility

- Two new refusals: `422 secret_path` and `503 redaction_failed`. The Pi adapter keeps
  its own result for both, and does not pause capture.
- A retained original that held secrets now holds markers in their place.
  `raw_sha256` and `raw_bytes` describe the retained, redacted bytes, as before.
  `capture.body_sha256` is still the digest of the body as received.
- The 16 MiB limit on a retained original applies to the redacted bytes. A marker is
  longer than a short secret, so output made of nothing but short secrets can reach
  the limit (`413`) before 16 MiB of input: 16 MiB of `secret=x` lines redacts to
  55 MiB.
